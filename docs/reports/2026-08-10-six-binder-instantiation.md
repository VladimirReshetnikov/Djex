# Six-binder instantiation across Djinn and Exference

Date: 2026-08-10

## Outcome

Djex now admits complete context-free leading `forall` chains of one through
six binders at every bounded instantiation entrance:

- Djinn query-local hypotheses, including the historical, query-closed, and
  query-correlated positive-only families;
- retained loaded Djinn schemes;
- Djinn provider-local scalar reconstruction and exact ordered assignments;
- Exference query-derived visible provider specialization; and
- Exference exact ordered assignments.

The public contract is now:

```haskell
maximumProviderInstantiationArguments = 6
```

Seven or more leading binders remain outside this finite capability. Checked
assignment runners reject a seven-argument vector before entering any of its
arguments. A Djinn miss involving an otherwise eligible retained seven-binder
scheme remains `NoEvidence`, because the uninstantiated opaque scheme is still
an honesty witness against a logical refutation.

## One shared bound

The synthesis foundation continues to export one argument limit to Djinn,
Exference, and downstream frontends. The private workers do not carry a new
literal six: eligibility tests, lazy list guards, scalar tuple reconstruction,
exact-vector validation, and public diagnostics all consult
`maximumProviderInstantiationArguments`.

Each checked assignment runner observes at most the first over-limit list cell
before entering an argument. The outer scalar and assignment association lists
remain capped at 32. The independent productive kind preflight remains capped
at 129 `GroundKind` nodes per argument, so increasing vector width does not
broaden or weaken the higher-kinded trust boundary.

## Generic tuple scheduling

The fair scheduler introduced for wide four-binder schemes was already
arity-generic. Six-binder historical, query-closed, query-correlated, and
loaded-scheme instances now use the same stable round-robin mixture of:

- contiguous source-order windows;
- repeated diagonal tuples;
- sparse monotone selections from both source-order directions; and
- the lexical Cartesian stream as a bounded fallback.

One- through three-binder schemes retain their historical lexical order.
Six-binder widening does not increase any search allowance: each eligible
scheme still exposes at most sixteen retained axioms, each positive-only family
retains at most 64 axioms, and tuple production and family building keep their
independent 512-attempt limits. The existing query candidate cutoff and proof
fuel are also unchanged.

Djinn regressions exercise an ordinary six-binder hypothesis, a non-lexical
source-order specialization of an abstract six-argument constructor, and a
retained loaded six-binder provider. These cases demonstrate that the wider
arity uses the established fair schedule instead of obtaining a special-case
tuple ordering.

## Provider assignments

Scalar provider evidence can reconstruct six positions under the existing
finite candidate vocabulary and tuple caps. Exact legacy or kinded evidence
instead supplies one complete correlated vector and consumes it once. Exact
vectors therefore bypass Cartesian tuple reconstruction, just as they did at
smaller arities, while retaining the 32-assignment and 32-direct-premise
bounds.

Djinn and Exference both retain six specified visible type applications in
source order. Exference's separate query-derived route likewise admits a
six-binder vacuous provider without changing its 32-combination allowance.
Seven-binder scalar and exact inputs are rejected at the shared structural
boundary. Empty evidence remains exactly inert for every runner.

Nothing in this widening changes assignment meaning. Vectors remain keyed to
one exact retained provider, must match its complete leading arity, must be
closed and context-free at the provider-scheme level, and are never flattened
or recombined with sibling vectors. Kinded assignments still check every
argument at its frontend-attested positional `GroundKind`; legacy assignments
still infer those ground kinds from observable provider uses and default only
an unconstrained vacuous position to `Type`.

## Preserving rank-N frontier sentinels

The pairwise, triple, quadruple, and capped quintuple formula-frontier fixtures
previously used six-binder hypotheses as sentinels: five-binder instantiation
could not solve them, so a successful proof isolated the intended open/opaque
formula plan. With six-binder instantiation available, those sentinels now use
seven leading binders.

Only the adjacent binder-chain width changes. The number and polarity of
recorded `forall` occurrence sites are unchanged, as are the structural plan
order and all plan caps. The GHC-facing quartic and quintic integration
fixtures likewise use seven-binder transports, so successful compilation still
demonstrates the intended rank-N frontier rather than the newly widened
instantiation rule.

This widening does not add a sextic formula frontier. The documented
twelve-site six-open/six-opaque occurrence gap remains outside the bounded plan
family; it is independent of the number of binders in any one adjacent
quantifier chain.

## Verification boundary

The regression set covers:

- the exported shared constant at six;
- Djinn query-local, non-lexical, loaded-scheme, and exact-assignment success;
- Exference query-derived and exact-assignment success;
- six visible arguments retained in order by the public facade;
- lazy seven-argument rejection by both checked adapters;
- conservative Djinn behavior for an eligible-looking seven-binder scheme;
  and
- GHC compilation of the widened rank-N frontier witnesses.

## Deliberate limits

This remains bounded instantiation, not general impredicative inference or
higher-rank subsumption. Contextual provider schemes remain ineligible,
constrained hypothesis-side chains stay opaque, and candidate vocabularies are
finite. A useful six-tuple can still fall outside the unchanged attempt or
retained-axiom windows. Seven or more leading binders remain unsupported, and
an empty search at any incomplete boundary remains inconclusive rather than a
proof of uninhabitability.
